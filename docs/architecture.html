EC2 Streaming Architecture

The 2-hourly describe-instances batch, rebuilt as a stream. Follow the numbered steps to trace one EC2 state change from AWS to an AI triage. Every credential on the way comes from OpenBao.

One EC2 stop, step by step

#What happensLatency
1AWS emits the state change; EventBridge delivers it to SQS.< 1 s
2The bridge validates it, writes Avro keyed by instance_id with acks=all, then deletes it from SQS. At-least-once.< 1 s
3All three Flink jobs read it. Duplicates are dropped on event_id; the watermark trails event time by 30 s.ms
4Joined with the CMDB row valid at event time and committed to Iceberg on the next checkpoint.30–60 s
5Upserted into instance_current_state with the current owner and the hour's transition count.1–2 s
6The third stop/start cycle within 10 minutes completes the pattern, and the alert is published in a Kafka transaction.30–60 s
7The triage agent reads only committed alerts and skips stale or already-triaged ones.ms
8It investigates through MCP: pipeline health, current state, Iceberg timeline, fleet activity.seconds
9Claude returns a structured triage, written to Postgres and ec2.alerts.triaged; then the offset is committed.~30 s
